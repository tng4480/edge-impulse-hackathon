# Judge Q&A (25 Hard Questions)

Short, honest answers grounded in `docs/results.md`, `docs/stress_results.md`, `docs/research/speech.md`, and cited primary sources. Never bluff; say plainly what is unverified.

---

### 1. Does it beat NEWS2?
**No — not on catch rate at equal alarm time, and we say that up front.** On 476 sepsis and 7,422 non-sepsis held-out PhysioNet 2019 test patients (with cutoffs picked strictly on validation patients), matching NEWS2's catch rate (78% vs 76% in the 24 h before onset) gives **43% fewer separate false alerts per patient-day** (0.91 vs 1.61) and **3 hours earlier** median warning (20 h vs 17 h), because 24 h trends don't bounce hour to hour. The trade-off is longer alert duration (22% vs 16% of non-sepsis hours), so at equal total alarm time NEWS2 catches more (76% vs 70%). That is why the device runs partial NEWS2 *alongside* the model rather than replacing it (`docs/results.md`).

### 2. Why not just use the watch's own sepsis feature?
**It doesn't have one.** Consumer smartwatches — including the Samsung Galaxy Watch 8 — do not screen for, detect, or diagnose sepsis, and Samsung explicitly designates its Health Sensor SDK metrics (HR, spot SpO₂, skin temperature) for general wellness, not diagnosis ([Samsung Health Sensor SDK](https://developer.samsung.com/health/sensor/overview.html); `wearos/README.md`). A watch also cannot measure reliable continuous chest respiratory rate, cannot enforce clinical pathway overrides like NICE CG151 neutropenic fever, and does not hand a tracked SBAR/FHIR task to an accountable nurse.

### 3. What about SpO₂ inaccuracy on darker skin?
**It is a real safety hazard, and neither our model nor NEWS2 corrects it.** Sjoding et al. (*N Engl J Med* 2020) showed pulse oximeters miss hypoxaemia (SaO₂ < 88% when SpO₂ reads 92–96%) roughly three times as often in Black as in White patients ([doi:10.1056/NEJMc2029240](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2029240)), which prompted the FDA's January 2025 draft guidance requiring larger, skin-tone-diverse clinical validation ([FDA Pulse Oximeters](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/products-and-medical-procedures/pulse-oximeters)). In our stress lab, a simulated +4 point SpO₂ over-read drops NEWS2's catch rate by 5 points (76% → 71%); our model's catch rate rises slightly (78% → 80%), which we explicitly disclose is **not** true robustness — it is likely an ICU supplemental-oxygen artefact (`docs/stress_results.md`). Our real mitigation is relying on melanin-invariant chest respiratory rate, labelling SpO₂ age, and never applying an unvalidated numeric skin-tone offset; prospective paired validation across skin tones is required before clinical claims.

### 4. Is this a medical device?
**Not today — right now it is an unvalidated decision-support prototype, and we never call it a diagnostic or medical device.** To deploy clinically in the UK, software that scores deterioration risk or guides escalation would almost certainly be regulated as **Software / AI as a Medical Device (SaMD / AIaMD)** under the UK MHRA framework ([MHRA Software and AI as a Medical Device Change Programme](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/software-and-ai-as-a-medical-device-change-programme)). In addition, any NHS deployment requires **DCB0129** (manufacturer clinical risk management: Clinical Safety Officer, Hazard Log, Clinical Safety Case) and **DCB0160** (deploying NHS organisation's local safety case) ([NHS England Digital Clinical Risk Management Standards](https://digital.nhs.uk/services/clinical-safety/clinical-risk-management-standards)). Our hash-chained audit log and explicit staleness/override design are early engineering groundwork for that safety case, not compliance.

### 5. Why Edge Impulse?
**Because it gave us a unified, reproducible offline runtime (`.eim`) on the Arduino UNO Q's Linux aarch64 core across two distinct sensor modalities.** We used Edge Impulse's **Bring Your Own Model (BYOM)** pipeline to deploy our LightGBM trend model (compiled via Hummingbird GEMM to ONNX because the free plan can't build classical-ML models natively), and a second Edge Impulse project for a native Audio MFCC → Keras `int8` spoken-digit model (`digits.eim`, 89.6% val accuracy on speaker-disjoint Speech Commands v2). Both run locally with zero cloud calls (`docs/pitch/edge_impulse_story.md`).

### 6. Why not run everything in the cloud?
**Three reasons: reliability, privacy, and latency.** Home Wi-Fi and mobile coverage drop, especially for elderly or rural patients; if inference lives in the cloud, an internet outage blinds the safety net right when someone deteriorates. Keeping `.eim` inference, baseline tracking, pathway rules, and the SQLite audit log on the UNO Q LAN enclave means monitoring continues offline, raw physiological streams never leave the home, and a paramedic can still scan an offline FHIR R4 QR code at the doorstep even with zero signal.

### 7. What's your data?
**Public, retrospective ICU data — not home wearable data.** The sepsis model is trained and evaluated on the **PhysioNet / Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2019** dataset (CC BY 4.0, [physionet.org/content/challenge-2019/1.0.0](https://physionet.org/content/challenge-2019/1.0.0/)), split strictly by patient MD5 hash: 184 sepsis / 2,926 non-sepsis validation patients (used only for early stopping and cutoff selection) and 476 sepsis / 7,422 non-sepsis held-out test patients. The spoken-digit model uses speaker-disjoint **Google Speech Commands v2** (CC BY 4.0, [arXiv:1804.03209](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03209)). ICU patients are sicker, nurse-charted, and already receiving treatment; home performance is **unverified** and requires prospective data.

### 8. What happens when it's wrong?
**We design for both failure directions.**
- **False negative (missed deterioration):** The model never gets veto power. Partial NEWS2 runs in parallel; hard clinical rules (e.g., typed oral temperature ≥ 38.0 °C after chemotherapy per [NICE CG151](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg151)) trigger immediately; and a one-tap *"I feel unwell / something is wrong"* button (inspired by [NHS England's Martha's Rule](https://www.england.nhs.uk/patient-safety/marthas-rule/)) escalates regardless of what the score says.
- **False positive (unneeded alert):** The device never diagnoses or prescribes; it opens a review task with SBAR context, vitals ages, and recent 6 h trends so a named nurse can triage quickly and log an outcome (`advised_111`, `home_visit`, `no_action`).

### 9. Who pays?
**NHS Virtual Ward / Hospital-at-Home services and acute oncology / post-surgical discharge teams — not consumers buying a gadget.** Virtual wards already fund short-term loaner monitoring kits for finite high-risk windows (e.g., 7–14 days post-chemotherapy or post-surgery), which also solves long-term wear fatigue ([NHS England virtual ward guidance](https://digital.nhs.uk/data-and-information/information-governance/guidance/virtual-wards)). Unit economics and NHS tariff reimbursement for this specific kit are **unverified**; a shadow pilot must first prove whether fewer separate interruptions actually save nurse review time without missing deterioration (noting the PVC-RAM-1 surgical RPM trial found no significant gain in days alive at home: [PubMed 34593374](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34593374/)).

### 10. What did you try that failed?
**Three concrete things we tested and rejected or disclosed honestly:**
1. **Slurred-speech (dysarthria) classifier on TORGO:** Leave-one-speaker-out evaluation gave only **0.635 clip AUC**, dropped when holding microphone fixed (0.669 head mic vs 0.592 array mic — recording-setup bias noted in [arXiv:2509.19946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19946)), scored a healthy control (`MC04`: 0.64) higher than 3 of 8 dysarthric speakers, and missed dysarthric `F03` on 93% of clips. We killed it and pivoted to a spoken-digit attention check (`docs/research/speech.md`).
2. **Dense neural network in Edge Impulse (8 h raw vitals):** Scored **0.698 ROC AUC** (15% sensitivity at 5% false alarms), beaten by 24 h LightGBM trends (**0.745 AUC**, 24% at 5% FA) (`docs/results.md`).
3. **Training without core temperature (v3) and equal-alarm-hour parity:** Even retrained without temperature, the model catches **60% vs NEWS2's 73%** at equal alarm time, or spends **32% vs 15%** of hours in alert at matched catch rate (`docs/results.md`). We kept NEWS2 running alongside it instead of hiding the result.

### 11. Wrist skin temperature isn't core temperature — doesn't that break your model?
**Yes, it is our biggest hardware limitation.** Wrist skin temperature can even *drop* at fever onset due to peripheral vasoconstriction, so our board deliberately **never** feeds wrist skin temperature into NEWS2 or the model (`board/sepsis-monitor/python/main.py`); it shows it only against the wearer's own baseline as context. Without an oral thermometer entry, the deployed v1 model fills the population median (36.9 °C), and our stress lab shows catch rate drops **22 points (78% → 55%)**, whereas 3-vital NEWS2 drops only 3 points (76% → 73%) (`docs/stress_results.md`). That is why the chemo pathway takes a typed oral thermometer reading and why a core-capable sensor (e.g., axillary/tympanic check-in) is needed clinically.

### 12. Why did you exclude "hours since ICU admission" if it raised AUC to 0.84?
**Because it is a classic dataset artefact (leakage of care setting, not physiology).** In PhysioNet 2019, longer ICU stays correlate with sepsis risk, so feeding `ICULOS` boosts retrospective AUC to 0.84 (`docs/results.md`) — but "hours in an ICU bed" does not exist at home and would collapse on real patients. Excluding it lowered our headline number and kept the evaluation honest.

### 13. Why does the live model need 8 hours of history before answering?
**Because it scores multi-hour physiological trends (`features.py` computes up to 24 h stats, 1 h/6 h deltas, and linear slope; `MIN_HOURS = 8`).** During the first 8 hours after setup, the model clearly reports `collecting hourly history (N/8 h)` rather than guessing, while partial NEWS2, personal resting baseline collection, and all human/pathway escalation rules are active immediately from minute one.

### 14. What about patients on beta-blockers or with COPD?
**Beta-blockers hurt our model substantially more than NEWS2.** In our stress lab, capping heart rate at 80 bpm drops model catch rate by **11 points (78% → 67%)** versus only **3 points** for NEWS2 (76% → 73%), because the model leans heavily on heart-rate trends (`docs/stress_results.md`). For COPD (hypercapnic respiratory failure), RCP NEWS2 specifies **SpO₂ Scale 2 (88–92% target)** ([RCP NEWS2](https://www.rcplondon.ac.uk/projects/outputs/national-early-warning-score-news-2)); our prototype currently implements Scale 1 only, so COPD Scale 2 support is a required clinical addition before any real-world pilot.

### 15. Why two devices (Galaxy Watch + UNO Q chest strap)? Who will wear a chest strap at home?
**Because wrist PPG alone does not give reliable continuous respiratory rate, and respiratory rate is one of the earliest, skin-tone-invariant signs of sepsis.** Asking healthy people to wear a chest strap forever won't work — which is why our target wedge is a **finite 7–14 day high-risk window** (post-surgery or post-chemotherapy nadir), where patients already tolerate wound dressings and home thermometers. Hardware-wise, the UNO Q + Modulino Movement is a hackathon prototype; a real clinical kit would shrink the chest sensor into a small washable BLE patch talking to a bedside hub.

### 16. Sepsis causes low blood pressure — how can you score NEWS2 without a BP cuff?
**We can't score full NEWS2, and the UI states that explicitly on every screen.** Our dashboard runs a **4-vital partial NEWS2** (RR, SpO₂, temperature, HR) and prints *"blood pressure, consciousness and oxygen are not measured"* (`board/sepsis-monitor/python/news2.py`, `dashboard.html`). In FHIR exports, unmeasured vitals carry formal `dataAbsentReason` codes (`fhir.py`). For a real virtual-ward kit, a £20–£30 Bluetooth oscillometric cuff prompted only when an alert or check-in fires would fill the BP gap without requiring continuous wear.

### 17. Why didn't you ship a slurred-speech classifier?
**Because our own leave-one-speaker-out test proved it didn't generalise.** On TORGO (16,544 clips, 15 speakers), MFCC + LightGBM achieved only 0.635 clip AUC, showed microphone-hardware bias, flagged healthy control `MC04` above 3 of 8 dysarthric speakers, and missed dysarthric `F03` on 93% of clips (`docs/research/speech.md`). Moreover, chronic dysarthria (cerebral palsy/ALS) is not acute sepsis confusion, and acute slurring is a stroke red flag needing 999. Switching to **backwards-digits recognition** directly mirrors bedside delirium screening for NEWS2 "C" (new confusion), with a tap/carer fallback.

### 18. Isn't your live replay case (`p112060`) cherry-picked?
**It is explicitly labelled as illustrative, not typical.** `p112060` was selected as the median-lead case among 28 qualifying held-out test patients where the model alerted ≥ 4 h before onset and neither method had already fired earlier (`board/sepsis-monitor/python/replay_cases.json`). We pair it with a negative control (`p000147`, never septic, neither alerts) and put the full 7,898-patient held-out test table on Slide 5 so judges see the true population numbers.

### 19. How do you stop walking or housework from triggering false alarms?
**Two layers today, and a clear remaining gap.** Live on the chest IMU, `resp.py` computes fast-motion standard deviation and refuses to report respiratory rate whenever motion exceeds `0.03 g`, and `liveness.py` only logs personal resting HR/HRV baselines when motion is below `0.012 g`. However, our stress lab shows that a simulated 2-hour exertion burst (+25 bpm HR, +6/min RR) still adds **+0.20 false alerts/day** to the model and **+0.29** to NEWS2 (`docs/stress_results.md`). Gating HR/RR trend features directly by chest activity state is a priority for the next iteration.

### 20. What happens if the patient is confused or alone and misses the alert?
**Unacknowledged alerts escalate automatically, and a carer can trigger review at any time.** If a wearer does not acknowledge an alert within the configured window (`escalate_after_s`, default 120 s), the system opens the **Carer SBAR view** with plain-English red flags (999 / 111 guidance) and opens an urgent task in the nurse handoff queue (`main.py`, `handoff.py`). Because confused or elderly patients may not complete a voice check, the check-in supports carer entry and care-home soft signs (RESTORE2-style).

### 21. How do you prevent alarm fatigue for nurses?
**By deduplicating episodes, tracking ownership, and measuring alarm hours honestly.** First, `handoff.py` enforces idempotency per episode (`pending → accepted → resolved`), so repeated alert ticks never flood the queue and two nurses can't duplicate work. Second, at matched catch rate the model cuts separate false-alert episodes by **43%** (0.91 vs 1.61/day). Third, we openly acknowledge that total hours in alert are higher (22% vs 16%), so our next evaluation must measure actual nurse review workload in shadow mode before claiming net workload savings.

### 22. How does it connect to NHS systems and handle UK GDPR?
**Offline-first FHIR R4 with zero patient-identifiable data stored on the board.** `fhir.py` builds a valid **HL7 FHIR R4 collection Bundle** (`Task`, five `Observation`s with LOINC/UCUM codes and UK SNOMED CT NEWS2 `1104051000000101`, plus `Provenance` naming the device and `.eim` engine; [HL7 FHIR R4 Task](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/task.html), [Observation](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/observation.html), [Provenance](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/provenance.html)). Because no `Patient` identity sits on the device, raw health data stays in the home LAN enclave and a paramedic can scan a compressed inline QR code (`SEPSISFHIR:...`, ≤ 1,000 chars) offline. Full NHS EPR integration (e.g., NHS England Booking and Referral / BaRS, identity, encryption, DTAC) is roadmap work, not built yet.

### 23. Your code mentions Modulino Thermo, Light, and Vibro — why aren't they working?
**We checked the hardware honestly: only Modulino Movement is detected on the Qwiic bus right now (`imu=1 thermo=0 light=0 vibro=0`).** We do not fake those readings or promise haptic buzzing on stage — the sketch gracefully reports missing modules and the dashboard displays `–` instead of inventing numbers.

### 24. How do you verify that the `.eim` on the board actually matches your offline benchmark?
**Bit-for-bit feature sharing and numerical parity checks.** Both offline training/evaluation (`training/strict_eval.py`) and the UNO Q app (`board/sepsis-monitor/python/main.py`) import the exact same pure-Python feature extractor (`board/sepsis-monitor/python/features.py`). In `training/to_onnx.py`, the Hummingbird GEMM ONNX export is checked against LightGBM over 1,000 validation rows (matching to 4 decimal places), and `replay_cases.json` embeds offline expected scores alongside live `.eim` re-scoring on the board (`replay.py`).

### 25. What is your next step after Sunday, and what evidence do you need?
**A prospective shadow-mode pilot on an NHS virtual ward or acute oncology discharge pathway — following [DECIDE-AI](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9116198/) early-stage clinical evaluation principles.** In shadow mode, alerts are logged silently alongside standard care while collecting paired home wearable readings, oral/tympanic core temperatures, medications (like beta-blockers), skin-tone diversity, and adjudicated clinical outcomes. Only once home calibration, core-temperature capture, and nurse review burden are proven prospectively should live clinical alerting be turned on under MHRA SaMD and DCB0129/0160 approval.
